This removes the even numbers. Or does it? An AI wrote it in two seconds, and it passes the sample test: one, two, three gives back one, three. Would you ship it? Try two, four, six, seven. You get four and seven back. Four is even. It survived. Here's why. Picture a row of seats, and you're checking tickets with your finger. Seat zero: two is even, so out it goes. Everyone slides one seat left. Your finger moves on to seat one. But four just slid into seat zero, and nobody checked it. Let's walk the code. The for loop keeps a hidden index, and it moves forward every time, no matter what. Remove deletes the value and shifts everything after it one step left. So the next item lands in the slot you just checked, and the loop skips it. Fix one: don't edit the list you're reading. Build a new one, keeping n only if it's odd. Fix two, in place: a read pointer and a write pointer. Read visits every item. When it finds a keeper, copy it to write, and move write forward. Write never passes read, so you never overwrite something you still need. Then cut off the tail. And even when remove works, it's slow: every remove shifts the whole tail. On a hundred thousand numbers, in our test run, looping with remove took eight seconds. The new list took four milliseconds. Python does catch this for a dictionary: runtime error, dictionary changed size during iteration. Lists and JavaScript arrays just skip, silently. Changing a collection while you loop over it?
