Watch binary search throw away half. Seven sorted numbers: one, three, five, seven, nine, eleven, thirteen. Find nine. You could check them one by one. Or do what you'd do with a paper dictionary. You don't read it page by page. You open the middle, see which half your word is in, and ignore the other half. Here goes. Lo starts at the left end, hi at the right. The middle is seven. Seven is less than nine, and the list is sorted, so everything up to seven is too small. Gone. Now the middle is eleven. Too big, so eleven and everything after it is gone. One number left: nine. Found, in three checks. Here's the code. Lo and hi mark the range where the answer could still be. While that range isn't empty, take the middle. If it's the target, return it. If it's too small, move lo past mid. If it's too big, move hi below mid. If the range runs out, the target isn't there. Every check halves the range, so it's O of log n. A million sorted numbers take at most twenty checks. A billion? Thirty. The classic bug: set lo to mid, instead of mid plus one. Search one, three, for three. Mid is zero, lo stays zero, and it loops forever. And it only works on sorted data, or anything where one comparison tells you which half to drop. Sorted data and a search?
