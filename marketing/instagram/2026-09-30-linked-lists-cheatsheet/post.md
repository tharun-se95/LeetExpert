## Alt text (per slide)

**Slide 1.** Cover. A leetExpert cheatsheet titled "Linked Lists", with a "Save this" tag. Tagline: "Pointer surgery — reverse, meet, and splice without losing the rest." Chips list what is inside: 5 smells to patterns, 5 patterns, 3 targets and 2 traps.

**Slide 2.** Smell to pattern. Reverse order of nodes → Iterative prev/curr/next. Middle or cycle? → Fast & slow. Remove nth from end → Leader gap of n. Merge two sorted lists → Dummy head walk. Reorder / rotate nodes → Cut + relink.

**Slide 3.** Pattern 1 of 5: Iterative reverse. When: Flip every next without recursion. Three pointers: prev, curr, next. Save next, rewire curr.next → prev, then advance. Return prev — that is the new head.

**Slide 4.** Pattern 2 of 5: Fast & slow. When: Middle node or cycle detection. Slow +1, fast +2. Middle when fast hits null; cycle when fast meets slow again. Draw one lap — the rhythm sticks.

**Slide 5.** Pattern 3 of 5: Dummy head. When: Delete or merge that might touch the real head. Fake node before the list so every splice shares one path. Return dummy.next. Eliminates special-case head deletes.

**Slide 6.** Pattern 4 of 5: Leader gap. When: Nth from end without knowing length first. Advance a leader n steps (or n+1 if you need the node before the cut). Move both until leader is null — follower sits at the edit point.

**Slide 7.** Pattern 5 of 5: Cut and relink. When: Rotate, split, or weave two chains. Find the cut with a length or two-pointer pass, then rewire heads/tails. Never lose the second half’s head before you attach it.

**Slide 8.** Targets, time and extra space. Reverse / middle / merge: O(n) time, O(1) space (Prefer iterative). Recursive reverse: O(n) time, O(n) space (Call stack depth). Cycle detect (Floyd): O(n) time, O(1) space (No visited set needed). Traps. Losing the next pointer: Save curr.next before rewiring. One missed save and the rest of the list is unreachable — silent data loss, not a crash. Off-by-one on the gap: Removing the nth-from-end needs the node before it. A gap of n vs n+1 is the usual bug; empty-list and n = length are the stress cases.

**Slide 9.** Closing slide: "The real Linked Lists cheatsheet, from the course." One of 21 cheatsheets in leetExpert, the interactive DSA course, each under its module's practice problems. leetexpert.com, link in bio.
