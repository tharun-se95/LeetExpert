Watch recursion go down and come back up. Factorial of three: three times two times one. Here's the recursive version. Think of a stack of trays in a cafeteria. Every call sets a fresh tray on top. You can only work on the top tray. Factorial of three can't finish yet: it needs factorial of two. So it waits, and a new frame goes on top. Two needs one. One needs zero. Zero is the base case: it just returns one. No more calls. Now it comes back up. The top frame pops and hands back one. One times one: one. Pop. Two times one: two. Pop. Three times two: six. The last frame pops, and the answer is six. That went four frames deep: n plus one. Here's the code. If n is zero, return one. That's the base case, the floor. Otherwise, return n times factorial of n minus one: a smaller problem, and the multiply waits until the call comes back. Every frame costs memory, and the stack is small. Python stops at a thousand frames: factorial of a thousand crashes with a recursion error. JavaScript allows around ten thousand. Now break the base case: step down by two instead of one. From three you go to one, then minus one, and you skip zero forever. It doesn't hang. It crashes: maximum recursion depth exceeded. Asked for the space of a recursive function?
