Max sum of k numbers in a row. Looks clean. An AI wrote it, and it passes the sample: two, one, five, one, three, two, with k of three, gives nine. Correct. Would you ship it? Try one, two, three, nine, with k of two. It says five. The answer is twelve: three plus nine. And when k is the whole length, it returns negative infinity. Here's why. Count the windows. Slide a frame two wide along four numbers. It fits at start zero, start one, and start two. That's three windows: n minus k, plus one. Let's walk the code. Best starts at negative infinity. For each start i, it sums k numbers and keeps the biggest. But range of n minus k stops one short. It only tries start zero and start one. The last window, three and nine, never gets checked. The fix is tiny: plus one. And there's a second problem. Every window is summed from scratch, k additions each time. That's O of n times k. Slide the frame instead. Sum the first window once. Then, each step, add the number entering on the right and subtract the one leaving on the left. Keep the best. One addition and one subtraction per step: O of n. In our test run, a hundred thousand numbers with k of ten thousand: summing every window took twelve seconds. Sliding took twelve milliseconds. See k in a row? Count your windows, and slide the sum.
