You've got a fifteen-dollar gift card. Spend every cent on exactly two things. The menu's sorted by price. Try every pair? Twenty-one combos. A thousand items? Almost half a million. Smarter: one finger on the cheapest, one on the priciest. One plus nine: ten. Too low. Here's the key: even with the priciest item, the one-dollar thing can't reach fifteen. Cross it off. Three plus nine, twelve. Gone. Four, thirteen. Five, fourteen. Seven plus nine, sixteen. Too high! Now nine's the problem. Cross it off. Seven plus eight… fifteen. Done. Six checks, not twenty-one. Every check crosses off an item: O of N time, O of one memory. Databases join sorted tables the same way: one pointer in each, no checking every pair. The catch? It has to be sorted. If not, sort first, or use a hash set. And stop when your fingers meet, or you'll buy the same item twice. Sorted input, looking for a pair? Say "two pointers" out loud.
