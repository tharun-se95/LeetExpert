Answer any range sum in O of one. Here's a list: two, four, one, five, three. What's the sum from index one to three? Four plus one plus five: ten. Easy. Now answer ten thousand of those. Adding up each range from scratch costs up to N every single time. Think of a car's odometer. It never tells you how long one trip was. It shows the total distance since the car was new. For any trip, subtract the reading at the start from the reading at the end. You never re-drive the trip. A prefix array is that odometer. It starts at zero. Add two: two. Add four: six. Add one: seven. Add five: twelve. Add three: fifteen. Here's the code. Prefix gets one more slot than nums, and slot zero holds zero: the sum of nothing. Then, for each x, the next slot is the previous slot plus x. One pass, O of N. To sum from l to r, take prefix at r plus one, minus prefix at l. Index one to three: twelve minus two, ten. Index two to four: fifteen minus six, nine. One subtraction, no matter how wide the range. In our test run, ten thousand queries on a hundred thousand numbers took five seconds the slow way. With a prefix array: six milliseconds, build included. The catch: the array has to stay fixed. Change one value, and every total after it goes stale. And watch the classic off by one: prefix at r, minus prefix at l, drops the last number. Lots of range sums on a list that doesn't change? Say prefix sums out loud.
