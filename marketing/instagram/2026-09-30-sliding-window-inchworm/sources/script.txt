If you see contiguous, think window. Here's the question: find the shortest stretch of this list that adds up to at least seven. Two, three, one, two, four, three. Checking every start and every end is O of N squared. Instead, picture an inchworm. The front stretches forward to grab new ground. When it's overreached, the back pulls in. Neither end ever backs up. Watch. The front grabs two, three, one: six. Not enough. It grabs two more: eight. That's at least seven, so record length four, then pull the back in. Three, one, two: six. Too small again. The front grabs four: ten. Pull in: one, two, four. That's seven, length three. Pull in again: six. The front grabs three: nine. Pull in: four, three. Seven, length two. That's the answer. Now the code. Right expands the window one step at a time and adds to the total. While the total is big enough, record the length, subtract the left number, and move left forward. Both pointers only move forward, so it's O of N, even with a loop inside a loop. On ten thousand numbers, in our test run, checking every stretch took eight hundred fifty milliseconds. The window took about one. The trap: write if instead of while, and the window shrinks at most once per step. It never gets tight. On this exact list it answers four, not two. And the window needs non-negative numbers. With negatives, shrinking can raise the total, and it misses answers. See contiguous, subarray, or substring? Say sliding window out loud. Your turn: target eleven, on one, two, three, four, five.
