Two pointers on one card. Save this. It starts with the smell. Sorted array, and you need a pair that hits a sum? Opposite ends. Left starts at the front, right at the back. If the sum is too small, move left in. Too big, move right in. Removing or moving things while you read ahead? Fast and slow. Fast explores. Slow marks the end of what you're keeping. When fast finds something new, slow steps forward and takes it. The biggest area between two lines? Move inward from both ends, and always move the shorter side, because moving the taller one can never raise the minimum. Merging two sorted lists? One pointer in each. Always take the smaller head, and when one list runs out, append the rest. Every one of these is a single pass, O of n, with O of one extra memory, and the merge is O of n plus m. Two traps. Opposite ends only works on sorted data: sort first, or pick another pattern. And watch the loop condition: left less than right. With less than or equal, the middle element can pair with itself.
